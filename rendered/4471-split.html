The two problems

parser: the shared parser reads every date month first. A tenant whose locale writes the day first gets its dates flipped. This is what #4471 is about, and it is the large majority of the reports, reported through the app, email and the form on booking-v2. Ticket: #1.

hint: the booking-v3 form tells people to type MM/DD/YYYY. Three tenants run that form (alder.physio, copper.studio, pale.harbour) and all three write the day first. Their people type what the hint says, month first, and the form reads it the tenant’s way. Only reports that came through the form on those tenants belong here. A parser fix does not change what the hint tells people to type. Ticket: #2.

Five of the parser reports read as a one-day move (a weekday off by one, an invite before the booking, Lisbon and Toronto getting different days for one meeting). The desk filed them under parser by tenant, in desk-notes.json, and the table counts them there. A time-zone cause is possible, not confirmed. The evidence is kept in #3.

What engineering decided

Engineering closed #4471 on 5 October:

  • parser: fixed, fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales. Ticket #1 is closed as completed.
  • hint: won’t fix, the booking-v3 form is retired. Ticket #2 is closed as not planned.

Why they were merged

Decision 0001 merges on the subject line alone. Subjects here are generic (“Wrong day on the invite”, “Date is wrong”), so both problems share them. Intake does not help either: its tag guesses from the reporter’s browser and not from the tenant, so it disagrees with the cause for a large part of the reports. The cause depends on the tenant (the locale it writes dates in, and the booking form it runs), as causes.ts already does.

The export also repeats some reports: its pages overlap, so a report can come twice. The counts here use one row per id.

What to do next time

  • Count reports per cause, not per subject, when you merge a big incident.
  • Check a merged ticket against the tenant’s locale and form before you mark it known.
